Algoverve — live

Options trading for people who don’t want to write code.

Traders build multi-leg strategies in a builder, test them against real market data, and then let them run on their own. The platform prices the positions, holds capital against exchange margin rules, and shows profit and loss moving as it moves.

Work

Six systems. Four are mine, two I only worked on.

I’ve marked which is which, because engineers who claim a whole team’s codebase are usually hiding something. This is scope only. The methods stay private, but if you want the detail I’ll go as deep on a call as an NDA lets me.

Built by me I contributed to it
01

Options execution engine

Built by me

The biggest thing here, and the one I’d want to be judged on. It runs live option strategies from end to end: schedules them, picks the instruments, fires the broker orders, protects them, and watches every open position against its risk rules. Seventeen worker processes have to keep agreeing with each other all day.

If it fires an order twice, that money is gone. If it misses an exit it was told to take, same. There is no version of this that is mostly working.

02

Live streaming gateway

Built by me

One connection per user, carrying their whole live trading picture to the browser. The happy path is the easy half. The real work is what happens when somebody’s train goes into a tunnel and their phone reconnects four minutes later, and they have to get back exactly what they missed. Nothing lost, nothing shown twice.

03

The streaming gateway before that one

Built by me

My first attempt at the same problem. It went to production and it worked, right up until the parts I’d got wrong started to matter. So I rewrote it, and number 02 is what came out.

I keep it on this list on purpose. An engineer who tells you they got a hard thing right first time is telling you they haven’t built many hard things.

04

Trading platform API

I contributed to it

The control plane the rest of the platform runs on. A user composes a strategy, this decides whether it’s valid, whether there’s margin for it, and whether that account is allowed it, then hands it to the engine.

Most of this codebase isn’t mine and I’m not going to pretend otherwise. My part is the strategy lifecycle, the handover into the engine, the risk limits, the payoff maths and the journal APIs.

05

Market data ingestion

I contributed to it

Holds a live broker feed for roughly 1,800 instruments and turns the incoming ticks into the price stream every other service reads. Another team codebase. Mine is the fast price path and the session timing.

06

Options backtesting engine

Built by me

My own project, built in my own time. A store of Indian index-options history and an engine that replays a strategy across it, so you can find out whether an idea was ever any good before you put money behind it. It’s also where I try things I wouldn’t try first on something live.

Everything above except number 06 is in production right now.

What I do

Five things I’m good at, and one I’m not.

If your problem isn’t on this list, I’d rather say so on the first call than work it out slowly on your budget.

Live data into a browser

One connection, updates that stay in order, and a reconnect that gives the user back exactly what they missed. Not more, not less.

Event-driven backends

Multi-worker pipelines that handle each item once, survive a restart without redoing work, park whatever keeps failing, and come back cleanly after a crash halfway through something.

Market data and trading systems

The specialist one. Live feeds, strategy scheduling, order execution, broker integrations, and cost modelling that matches what the exchange actually charges you.

Making a slow system fast

Most of my last year has been taking things that already worked and making them several times faster. The answer is almost never a rewrite, which is usually the news people don’t want.

APIs your front-end dev won’t fight

A clear contract, written down, that doesn’t change under them on a Friday. I’ve been on the other end of the bad version of this.

Not front-end, not design

I’d do a mediocre job and charge you for it. I’ll define the API cleanly instead, so whoever does your front-end has an easy time.
