01 — Execution

Options execution engine

A multi-process engine that runs live option strategies end to end: schedules them, selects instruments, fires and protects real broker orders, and monitors every open position continuously against its risk rules.

Seventeen independent worker processes coordinate through a shared event backbone. The engineering challenge isn't throughput, it's certainty: several workers can reach the same conclusion at the same instant, and exactly one action must result. Equally, a process that dies mid-order must never leave a live position the system can no longer see.

It's built to be operated, not just deployed — health checks per process, a full metrics surface, isolation for poisoned work, and deliberate failure drills that kill workers to prove recovery actually happens.

02 — Real-time

Live streaming gateway

One authenticated connection that carries a user's entire live trading picture into the browser — positions, profit and loss, and margin — and holds up when the network doesn't.

Streaming data is easy. Streaming it correctly is the job. A phone that drops into a lift for ninety seconds must come back to a complete and accurate picture, never a silently incomplete one — and one transient upstream hiccup must not cascade across every connected user at once.

It also refuses to guess. Where a value can't be established with confidence, the system withholds it rather than publishing a number a trader might act on. That rule is enforced in code, not in documentation.

03 — Quantitative

Margin & analytics engine

Regulatory-grade portfolio margin computed from exchange-published risk data, so the capital the platform reserves matches what a real broker would block — across two exchanges, refreshed through the trading day.

Approximating margin is how a platform over-commits a client's capital or under-reserves against a position it can't cover. This computes it properly, from the primary source, and holds a consistent view even while the underlying data is being refreshed underneath it.

Around it sits the rest of the quantitative surface: an options pricing and greeks suite, and a cost model that accounts for every statutory charge separately rather than as a lumped estimate.

04 — Platform

The wider platform

The trading core is one part of a larger product. I work across the services that surround it, and I know how the whole system fits together.

A control-plane API where users compose strategies and hand them to the engine — I own the strategy lifecycle and the seam that projects a saved strategy into the live runtime, plus risk limits, the trading-journal APIs and the payoff service. Alongside it, a market-data service that holds a live exchange feed and turns it into the price stream everything downstream consumes.

The platform also runs subscription payments and checkout, automated statutory invoice generation, and transactional email — built by the team I work in, and part of the product I help operate end to end.
